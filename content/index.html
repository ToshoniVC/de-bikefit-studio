Landing page

The landing page is the first thing a visitor sees. Its job is to land the promise in one line, show that this studio is for them (whoever they are), and let them hear from people who already came through the door.

1. Hero — catchphrase

Ride with comfort. Life's too short to bike with pain.

One line, no subtitle. The whole point of the brand sits here. The button under it leads to the booking page.

2. Who it's for — personas

A horizontal row of five personas, each with a short pitch. The first one (children) gets a little more — it's the one most parents are uncertain about.

01 · Young riders

Kids grow fast — a bike that fits today is off by next season, and a growing body learns whatever you put it through. For junior racers already clocking real hours in the saddle, a poor fit doesn't just cost watts on race day; it locks in habits that follow them into adulthood.

We track the fit as they grow. Affordable, clear, on their terms.

02 · Touring cyclists

Long days, heavy bags, distant horizons — a fit you trust at kilometre 200 makes the trip what it should be.

03 · Recreational riders

For weekend rides that should leave you fresh, not aching the next morning.

04 · Daily commuters

Five days a week, rain or shine. A fit that holds up to the daily grind — and the cold hands and the wet seat.

05 · Family pack

Mum, dad, kids — one studio session, every bike fitted. Everyone rides home better.

3. Success stories · reviews

A short block of real voices. Three to five reviews, rotating. Each one names the kind of rider (recreational, commuter, racer, parent of junior, etc.) so a visitor sees themselves.

Reviews below are placeholder copy. Replace with real client testimonials before launch — ideally with first name, age, and the type of riding they do. Keep them short (one paragraph max).

4. Closing CTA

One final button at the bottom of the page, same destination as the hero: Book your fit. Short line above it tying back to the catchphrase — e.g. "Comfort is a choice. Make it."
